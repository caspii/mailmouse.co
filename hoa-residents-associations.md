## The communication problem every association has

An HOA (homeowners' association) or residents' association has two jobs. The board must discuss decisions privately. Every resident must hear about those decisions. Most associations handle both with a messy mix of tools:

- **Personal email accounts.** The chair emails from their own Gmail. When they step down, the contacts and history go with them.
- **Long CC lists.** Every resident's address is visible to everyone, which causes privacy complaints.
- **Chat groups.** A WhatsApp or Facebook group reaches some residents and misses the rest. Arguments spread fast.
- **Paper notices.** They are slow and expensive, and there is no record of who received what.

## Two lists, two jobs

Most associations need just two lists to start:

| List | Type | Who posts | Who receives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `board@yourhoa.org` | Discussion | Board members | Board members only |
| `residents@yourhoa.org` | Announcement | Board only | Every household |

Residents can **reply to the board** without the reply going to all residents. That stops reply-all arguments before they start.

Add more lists when you need them, for example `building-b@`, `garden-committee@` or `social@`.

## Built for committees that change

Boards change every year or two. MailMouse is built for that:

- **Co-owners on every list.** No single person holds the keys.
- **One-click handover** at the AGM (annual general meeting). The new board takes over members, settings and history.
- **A record of what was sent.** Every announcement is archived, so you can show when residents were told about a fee change or a works notice.

## Questions associations ask

**Can residents unsubscribe from official notices?**
Every message includes an unsubscribe link, as email law requires. Your bylaws may say how official notices must be delivered — check them before relying only on email.

**Can tenants and owners be on different lists?**
Yes. Create separate lists, or put the same person on several.

**Can we use our own domain?**
Yes. Use an address like `board@maplecourt.org`, or a free `@mailmouse.co` address.

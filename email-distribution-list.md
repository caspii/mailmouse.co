An **email distribution list** is one email address that delivers a message to many people. You send to `team@example.com`, and every person on the list receives a copy. You don't type or remember their individual addresses.

The term comes from Microsoft Exchange and Outlook, where IT departments create distribution lists for company teams. Today people use it for any shared group address.

## Distribution list, contact group or mailing list?

Three tools get called "distribution lists." They work differently.

| | Contact group | Distribution list | Mailing list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | In your own address book | On your company's mail server | On a mailing list service |
| Who can send to it | Only you | Usually anyone in the company | You decide: anyone, members, or organisers only |
| Has its own address | No | Yes | Yes |
| Works for people outside your company | Yes, but only you can use it | Often blocked or limited | Yes |
| Members can unsubscribe themselves | No | No | Yes |
| Keeps an archive | No | No | Usually |
| Typical use | Your personal group of friends | A department inside one company | Clubs, churches, schools, communities |

**Quick rule:**
- Emailing the same few people yourself? Use a **contact group**.
- Everyone works at the same company? Use your company's **distribution list**.
- Members use different email providers, or more than one person needs to send? Use a **mailing list**.

## How to create a distribution list in Gmail

Gmail has no true distribution list. You have two options:

1. **A contact label.** In Google Contacts, select people and click **Manage labels → Create label**. In Gmail, type the label name in the To field. Only you can use it. See our [Gmail group email guide](/how-to-create-a-group-email-in-gmail/) for the steps.
2. **Google Groups.** This creates a real group address. Members need Google accounts for some features, and messages often go to spam. See [Google Groups alternative](/google-groups-alternative/).

## How to create a distribution list in Outlook

- **Outlook (personal):** create a **contact list** (older versions call it a "contact group"). Only you can use it.
- **Microsoft 365 (business):** an admin creates a **distribution list** in the Microsoft 365 admin center under **Teams & groups → Active teams & groups → Distribution lists**, then **Add a distribution list**. It gets its own address that anyone in the company can use.

See our [Outlook email group guide](/how-to-create-an-email-group-in-outlook/) for step-by-step instructions.

## How to create a distribution list with a mailing list service

A mailing list service works with every email provider. The steps are usually:

1. **Choose an address**, such as `members@yourclub.org`.
2. **Add members** by pasting addresses or uploading a spreadsheet.
3. **Choose who can post:** everyone, members only, or organisers only.
4. **Send** to the list address from your normal email.

## Common problems with distribution lists

**Messages go to spam.** When a list forwards a message, the receiving server may decide it is forged. Good list services rewrite the sender so the message passes. [Why group emails go to spam](/why-group-emails-go-to-spam/).

**Reply-all storms.** Someone replies to everyone, then others reply asking to be removed. Fix it with an announcement-only list, or set replies to go to the sender.

**Nobody owns the list.** The person who created it leaves. Always give a list at least two owners.

**External members can't send.** Company distribution lists often block messages from outside addresses. A mailing list service lets you allow them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a distribution list the same as a listserv?**
Nearly. "Listserv" usually means a discussion list where members reply to each other. "Distribution list" often means one-way sending. Many tools do both. See [What is a listserv?](/what-is-a-listserv/)

**How many people can be on a distribution list?**
It depends on the tool. Personal contact groups are limited by your email provider's sending limits, often a few hundred recipients a day. Mailing list services handle thousands.

**Can people outside my organisation join?**
With a mailing list service, yes. With a company distribution list, it depends on your IT settings.

**Do members see each other's addresses?**
Not on a proper list. Each person receives the message addressed from the list, without the other members' addresses.

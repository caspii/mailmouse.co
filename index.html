The four ways group email usually goes wrong

The giant CC list

Sixty addresses pasted into every email. Someone is always missing, and everyone can see everyone's address.

"It went to my spam"

Group emails from free list tools often land in junk — especially when members use Yahoo or a work address.

The secretary left

The only person with the password stepped down. Now nobody can add members or fix anything.

"I'm not joining WhatsApp"

Half the group lives in a chat app. The other half only reads email. Nobody sees the full picture.

Questions

Do my members need to sign up for anything?

No. You add their email addresses and they start receiving messages. Only organisers log in.

How is this different from Google Groups?

Members don't need Google accounts, there are no ads, data stays in the EU, and MailMouse is built to keep your messages out of spam. Read the full comparison.

Why do group emails go to spam?

Email providers check that a message really comes from the domain it claims. Mailing lists that forward messages often fail that check. MailMouse sends in a way that passes it. Read the explanation.

What happens when our organiser steps down?

Every list has at least two owners. The outgoing organiser hands over to someone else, and the list, members and history stay exactly as they were.

Can only some people post?

Yes. Make a discussion list where everyone can reply, or an announcement list where only organisers can send.

Can I use my own domain?

Yes. Use an address like members@yourclub.org, or a free @mailmouse.co address.
